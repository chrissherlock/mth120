Week 3 Overview Hub

Welcome to Week 3: From Stepping Stones to Fluid Motion

Over the past two weeks, you've conquered the discrete world. You looked at sequences as stepping stones, examining what happens as you jump from step $n$ to step $n+1$, and built powerful logical tools to trap limits at infinity. You have proven that you can handle the rigorous mechanics of real analysis.

Now, we shift gears. Instead of isolated points, we step into the continuous world of Functions. This week is all about mapping inputs to outputs in a fluid, connected way. We will look at how curves behave and how special functions (like logarithms and exponentials) stretch and compress numbers.

The Big Picture: Mapping the Continuum

If a sequence is a flipbook of individual snapshots, a function is a seamless movie.

In Lecture 7 (Functions), we lay the groundwork. We revisit domains and ranges, but with a more critical eye. We explore what it takes for a function to be reversible (injective and surjective properties) and look at the geometry behind inverse functions. This is the structural foundation you need before we bend these curves.

In Lecture 8 (Transcendental Functions), we step outside the algebraic sandbox of polynomials. We tackle exponential growth, demystify the natural logarithm (why does $e$ keep showing up?), and review the periodic, oscillating nature of trigonometric functions. You will see how to define functions that algebra alone cannot capture.
