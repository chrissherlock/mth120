Welcome to Week 2 of MTHS120. This unit bridges intuitive calculus and real analysis by formalising the concept of infinity and convergence across three dedicated lectures.

Bridging the Gap: From Intuition to Epsilon-N Rigor

In introductory calculus, we are often told that a limit is a value that a sequence "approaches" or "gets closer and closer to" as $n$ marches toward infinity. While that intuition helps picture motion, pure mathematics demands precision: exactly how close is "close," and after what point is that proximity permanently guaranteed?

This week transitions from intuitive hand-waving to rigorous analytical thinking across three focused lectures:

  • Lecture 4: Formal $\epsilon\text{-}N$ Convergence & Limit Laws: Turning limits into an exact challenge. You name any tiny tolerance $\epsilon > 0$, and the sequence produces a cutoff index $N$ beyond which every term stays trapped within that narrow window forever.
  • Lecture 5: The Monotone Convergence Theorem & Squeeze Principle: Proving that bounded monotonic sequences must settle down to their supremum, deriving Euler's number $e$, and trapping wild oscillatory sequences between converging bounds.
  • Lecture 6: Recursive Sequences & Divergence to Infinity: Discovering why blind algebra fails on recurrences without existence proofs, proving error contraction to $\sqrt{2}$, and testing unbounded growth with the $M\text{-}N$ towering floor test.
