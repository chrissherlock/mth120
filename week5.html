Week 5 Overview Hub

Welcome to Week 5: Activating the Machine

The launchpad we built with limits is about to ignite. In Week 4, we studied the rigorous edge of the mathematical abyss, learning to approach discontinuities and infinities using the $\epsilon\text{-}\delta$ telescope. We mapped static snapshots of unbroken curves.

This week, we hit the accelerator. We take that exact same limit machinery and aim it at a 300-year-old question: how do we measure instantaneous change? By shrinking the distance between two points on a secant line down to zero, we unlock the tangent line and officially enter the world of Differential Calculus.

The Big Picture: Moving from Secants to Optimisation

In Lecture 12 (Rates of Change & Derivatives), we bridge the gap between average and instantaneous speed. By shrinking the interval of a secant line down to exactly zero, we define the tangent line, solve the $0/0$ paradox, and officially give birth to the limit definition of the derivative.

Lecture 13 (Derivatives of Elementary Functions) moves us away from computing limits from first principles. Instead, we build our "Lego brick" rules for differentiation: the Power, Product, Quotient, and Chain rules, allowing us to compute instantaneous rates of change for any algebraic or transcendental function on the fly.

In Lecture 14 (Monotone Functions and Concavity), we learn to read a function's mind. By tracking the sign of the first derivative $f'(x)$ and the second derivative $f''(x)$, we can perfectly map where a curve is climbing, falling, or changing its structural bend (points of inflection).

In Lecture 15 (Application: Optimisation), we deploy our new analytical tools into the real world. We will hunt down absolute and relative extrema—the exact mathematical peaks and valleys (stationary points and critical points) where natural systems and business models operate at peak efficiency.
