Week 4 Overview Hub

Welcome to Week 4: The Calculus Frontier

Up until this point, mathematics has been a study of static states. You've evaluated functions at specific, fixed inputs and solved algebra for exact answers. But what happens when an input is strictly forbidden—like dividing by zero—yet we desperately need to know how the system behaves right next to it?

This week, we cross the boundary into dynamic mathematics. We introduce the Limit, master rigorous Continuity ($\epsilon\text{-}\delta$), explore the Intermediate Value Theorem (IVT), and analyze long-term behavior at infinity with Asymptotes.

The Big Picture: Navigating Limits, Continuity & Asymptotes

Lecture 9 (Continuity of Functions) dives into the core mechanics of unbroken curves. We explore error control through a factory machine analogy, set up the rigorous four-clause $\epsilon\text{-}\delta$ formal definition, analyze jump discontinuity jailbreaks, and master the Intermediate Value Theorem with root-finding applications.

In Lecture 10 (The Limit of a Function), we step back to formalize the notation $\lim_{x \to c} f(x)$. We look at left-hand and right-hand limits, removable discontinuities (holes), and algebraic techniques to evaluate indeterminate forms like $\frac{0}{0}$.

In Lecture 11 (Limits at Infinity & Asymptotes), we look at the far horizons of the coordinate plane. We use limits to formally identify horizontal and vertical asymptotes, tracking the ultimate end-behavior of rational and transcendental functions.
