Week 4 Overview Hub

Welcome to Week 4: The Calculus Frontier

Up until this point, mathematics has been a study of static states. You've evaluated functions at specific, fixed inputs. You've solved algebra for exact answers. But what happens when an input is strictly forbidden—like dividing by zero—yet we still desperately need to know how the system behaves right next to it?

This week, we cross the boundary into dynamic mathematics. We introduce the Limit—the foundational mechanism that powers all of calculus. By learning to track where a function is "intending" to go, rather than just where it arrives, we gain the ability to analyze jumps, bridge holes in our domains, and mathematically define what it means for a line to be truly continuous.

The Big Picture: Navigating Jumps and Holes

Lecture 9 (The Limit of a Function) kicks off our journey. We will formalize the notation $\lim_{x \to c} f(x)$ and learn how to evaluate functions as they approach a point from the left and the right. We will encounter removable discontinuities (holes) and vertical asymptotes, learning how algebraic manipulation can save us when direct substitution results in $\frac{0}{0}$.

In Lecture 10 (Continuity and the IVT), we leverage our new understanding of limits to construct a flawless mathematical definition of an "unbroken" curve. If a curve is continuous, it grants us a massive mathematical superpower: the Intermediate Value Theorem (IVT), which allows us to prove that equations have solutions even when we can't perfectly calculate them.

In Lecture 11 (Limits at Infinity & Asymptotes), we look at the far horizons of the Cartesian plane. What happens to a system as time $t \to \infty$? We will use limits to formally identify horizontal asymptotes and determine the ultimate, long-term behavior of rational and exponential functions.
